# Automation Warrior publishing plan
Prepared September 23, 2026

## Editorial direction

Write for founders, CEOs, COOs, and leadership teams of 7–9 figure businesses. The central promise is “Make AI work for your business.” Show how Stephen connects business priorities, practical implementation, and a more capable team.

Lead with the operating problem and the decision a leader needs to make. Explain the people, process, economics, and measurement before introducing a specific tool. Keep technical implementation guides available as supporting resources.

## The first case study

Use this structure:

1. Business context: industry, relevant operating scale, and the people involved.
2. Problem: the bottleneck and its cost or operational consequence.
3. Baseline: the measure, denominator, period, and source.
4. Decision: alternatives considered and why the chosen approach fit.
5. Implementation: what changed in the process, systems, and team responsibilities.
6. Adoption: how staff were trained and how exceptions were handled.
7. Outcome: a comparable period, quality checks, costs, and any limitations.
8. Attribution: other changes that may have contributed to the result.
9. Lesson: what Stephen would repeat or change.

Client names are optional. Useful detail and verifiable evidence matter more than a logo. Anonymized examples still need support and must respect the client’s confidentiality.

If the available evidence supports an implementation story but not a quantified outcome, publish the process and decision story at that narrower level. Do not fill missing figures with estimates presented as results.

## Useful credibility assets beyond articles

- A client-approved case-study PDF with a process diagram and a labeled before-and-after comparison.
- A short narrated walkthrough of a real workflow using approved or redacted information.
- A sample assessment deliverable: one-page opportunity brief, owners, measures, dependencies, and a pilot decision.
- A role-specific training worksheet that shows how the team practices and checks its work.
- A client quote used with permission, tied to a concrete engagement and outcome.

## A practical publishing sequence

1. Publish the three foundational business articles and retain the technical guide.
2. Develop the strongest evidence-backed case study next.
3. Follow with the growth retrospective and a team-training article.
4. Alternate a leadership decision piece with a delivery example or practical asset.

Aim initially for two substantial pieces per month. Use one primary article to support shorter posts or a walkthrough when appropriate. This is an editorial recommendation, not an automated publishing schedule.

## Standards for future content

- Write from Stephen’s actual experience, stated approach, or clearly labeled hypothetical examples.
- Show what the reader can decide or do differently after reading.
- Use specific process details, tradeoffs, ownership, and evidence.
- Explain how the change helps the existing team.
- Identify whether a number is measured, estimated, or illustrative.
- Link current technical claims to primary documentation and record the review date.
- Keep publication and meaningful update dates accurate.
- End with a next step matched to the topic: assessment, implementation and training, or ongoing fractional CAIO ownership.
- Verify internal links and the mobile reading experience before publishing.
- Do not invent clients, quotations, project histories, benchmarks, savings, or guarantees.
- Keep implementation mechanics in technical resources when they distract from the leader’s decision.
